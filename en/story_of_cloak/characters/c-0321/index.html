Barium

Barium metal has only a small number of applications in industry. It has been used in the past as a getter in vacuum tubes. Barium is a component of YBCO (a high-temperature superconductor) and electroceramics, and can also be added to steel to reduce the number of carbon particles in the metal’s makeup. Barium compounds are used to create the green color in fireworks. Barium sulfate is added to drilling fluids as an insoluble heavy additive, and in medicine it is used as an X-ray contrast agent. Soluble barium salts are toxic because they ionize into barium ions, and are therefore also used as rat poison.

Boron

Biologically, the toxicity of borate is low to mammals (equivalent to the impact of table salt); but it is high to arthropods and can be used as a pesticide. Boric acid has mild antimicrobial properties, and several boron-containing organic antibiotics are known. Boron is an essential element for plants. In agriculture, borax and boric acid can be used as fertilizers in trace amounts, but excessive amounts are toxic. Boron compounds play a role in strengthening plant cell walls. Although there is some evidence that it is helpful for bone mass, it is unknown whether it is an essential element for mammals (including humans).

Copper (English: Copper) is the earliest discovered chemical element. Its chemical symbol is Cu (from Latin: Cuprum[3]), its atomic number is 29, and its atomic weight is 63.546 u. Pure copper is a soft metal with a reddish-orange surface with a metallic luster when cut. It has good ductility, high thermal conductivity and electrical conductivity. Therefore, it is the most commonly used material in cables, electrical and electronic components. It can also be used as building materials and is composed of many alloys, such as silver for jewelry, white copper for making ship hardware and coins, and constantan for strain gauges and thermocouples. Copper alloys have excellent mechanical properties and very low resistivity, the most important of which are bronze and brass. Additionally, copper is a durable metal that can be recycled multiple times without losing its mechanical properties.

Copper is one of the metals that can be used directly in nature (natural metal), which made it used by people in many different areas in 8000 BC. Thousands of years later, copper became the first metal to be smelted from sulfide ore in 5000 BC; it became the first metal to be shaped in a mold in 4000 BC, and in 3500 BC it was forged with another metal tin to form the first alloy in human history - bronze [4].

Copper often forms divalent copper salts in nature, which give minerals such as azurite, malachite, and turquoise their blue or green color, and have also been widely used as pigments throughout history. Copper oxidized to a patina is often used on building roofs. Copper is sometimes used in decorative arts, either in elemental form or as a compound. In addition, copper compounds are used as bacteriostatic agents, fungicides, and wood preservatives.

Copper is an essential trace dietary mineral for all living things because it is an important component of the respiratory enzyme complex cytochrome c oxidase. In molluscs and crustaceans, copper is a component of the blood pigment hemocyanin, but in fish or other vertebrates, it is replaced by the iron-hemoglobin complex. In addition, in the human body, copper is mainly distributed in the liver, muscles and bones [5], and the average adult contains about 1.4 to 3.2 mg of copper per kilogram of body weight [6].

Zinc (English: Zinc) is a chemical element with the chemical symbol Zn, atomic number 30, and atomic weight 65.38 u. It is a transition metal. Zinc is also called sub-lead because its shape and color are similar to lead. It was also called Japanese lead and white lead in ancient times. Zinc is a hard and brittle substance at room temperature with a blue-silver luster. Zinc is the first element in group 12 of the periodic table of elements. In some respects, zinc is chemically similar to magnesium: both exhibit a single oxidation state (+2), and the Zn2+ and Mg2+ ions are similar in size. Zinc is the 24th most abundant element in the earth's crust and has five stable isotopes. The most common zinc-containing mineral is sphalerite, which is zinc sulfide. The largest mineable mineral deposits are in Australia, Asia and the United States. Zinc minerals are refined using froth flotation and are roasted and electrolytically refined.

*Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc mixed in various proportions. It has been used by people as early as 3,000 years ago in the Aegean Sea, Iraq, the United Arab Emirates, Kalmykia, Turkmenistan and Georgia, and 2,000 years ago in western India, Uzbekistan, Iran, Syria, Iraq and Israel/Palestine [2][3][4]. Although zinc metal was known in ancient Rome and Greece, it was not mass-produced in India until the twelfth century [5]. From minerals in Rajasthan we have conclusive evidence dating zinc production back to the sixth century BC [6]. So far, the oldest pure zinc comes from Zawar, Rajasthan, where distillation was used to produce pure zinc as early as the ninth century AD [7]. Alchemists burned zinc in the air to create zinc oxide, which they called "white snow." *

**This element may have been named by the alchemist Paracelsus in German: Zinke (fork, tooth). Pure metal zinc was discovered by the German chemist Margraf in 1746. In 1800, Luigi Galvani and Alessandro Volta revealed the electrochemical properties of zinc. The main application of zinc is corrosion-resistant iron galvanizing (hot-dip galvanizing). It plays an indelible role in the manufacturing of carbon-zinc batteries in modern industry. The most representative use is "galvanized iron sheet". This technology is widely used in various industries such as automobiles, electric power, electronics, and construction. Other applications include batteries, small non-structural castings and alloys such as brass. People commonly use various zinc compounds in their daily lives, such as zinc carbonate and zinc gluconate (dietary supplements), zinc chloride (deodorant), zinc pyrithione (anti-dandruff shampoo), zinc sulfide (fluorescent paint), and dimethyl zinc or diethyl zinc from organic laboratories. **

***Zinc is an essential mineral, including prenatal growth and postnatal development [8]. Approximately two billion people in developing countries are affected by zinc deficiency and its associated diseases [9]. If children are deficient in zinc, it will lead to growth retardation, late puberty, decreased immunity and diarrhea [8]. In biochemistry, zinc is a ubiquitous cofactor in enzymes, such as alcohol dehydrogenase in the human body [10]. ***
